# Operator Questions and Decisions

Use this policy whenever a workflow must ask the operator a question, request
clarification or approval, or surface a decision. It governs how necessary
questions are explained; it does not create new approval gates.

## Investigate before asking

Inspect the available repository, docs, contract, prior answers, command
results, and applicable project policy first. Resolve objective questions from
that evidence when the workflow permits it. Ask only for information or
authority that cannot be safely inferred, or at an approval gate the workflow
already requires.

## Make the decision understandable

Give a reasonable explanation in plain language, with detail proportional to
the decision. Do not force the explanation into a fixed template or length.
Ensure the operator can understand:

- where the question came from, including the inspected evidence or rule that
  exposed it;
- why the answer is needed now and what remains blocked or would change;
- the practical effect of the available options;
- a grounded current-project example, or a clearly labeled hypothetical when
  the project has no suitable example;
- the recommended choice and its evidence-based rationale, when the project
  context supports one; otherwise say that there is no strong recommendation;
  and
- the exact choice, clarification, or authorization being requested.

Include at least one example in every request; keep it brief for a simple
choice and expand it only when the decision needs more background. Examples
must come from the inspected project or its contract, or be clearly labeled as
hypothetical and used only to illustrate mechanics. Do not invent project
facts, imply that an example is evidence, or frame examples to steer the
operator toward an unsupported answer. Present material options fairly even
when recommending one.

## Preserve workflow autonomy

Do not turn routine, reversible, evidence-resolved work into operator
questions. Preserve the workflow's existing auto-resolution, risk, security,
and approval rules. When a worker cannot contact the operator directly, return
the same context as a compact decision packet to the coordinator instead of
guessing or asking an unexplained question.
